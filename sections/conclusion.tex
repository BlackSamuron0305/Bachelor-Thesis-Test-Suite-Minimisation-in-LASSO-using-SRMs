\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Summary}

This thesis developed an SRM-based test-suite minimisation framework for LASSO and addressed research questions RQ1--RQ4. A systematic literature review identified greedy heuristics---particularly Enhanced HGS---as the most suitable class of techniques. Using evaluation criteria C1--C7, the Mutation-Aware Enhanced HGS (MA-EHGS) variant was selected as the most effective approach, combining reduction capability, structural and behavioural preservation, scalability, and compatibility with LASSO's language-independent behavioural abstraction.

The implementation integrated MA-EHGS into LASSO's analysis pipeline, combining line, branch, and method coverages as well as mutation information within a unified SRM requirements representation. Empirical evaluation on benchmark systems achieved reductions of 46.9--55.6\% while preserving 100\% structural coverage for all configurations with $\alpha>0$ (i.e., whenever coverage receives non-zero weighting) and 100\% mutation retention for all configurations with $\beta>0$. This follows directly from the HGS selection mechanism, which never discards tests that introduce previously uncovered coverage requirements or kill previously unkilled mutants. Validation at unit-test level confirmed correct algorithmic behaviour and SRM integration, although full pipeline evaluation across diverse projects remains future work.

\section{Key Contributions}

The thesis makes three main contributions:

\paragraph{Algorithmic contribution.}
MA-EHGS extends the validated Enhanced HGS algorithm~\cite{gladston2015} with a dual-parameter weighting scheme that jointly considers structural coverage and mutation preservation:
\[
\text{score}(t) = \alpha \cdot |\text{newCoverage}| + \beta \cdot |\text{newMutants}|
\]
This enables configurable balancing between structural adequacy and behavioural (mutation-based) fault-detection preservation.

\paragraph{Practical integration into LASSO.}
The framework extracts metric data from SRM response objects, performs minimisation, and generates reduced SRMs while preserving schema and language independence.

\paragraph{Modular architecture.}
The separation of data extraction, scoring logic, and SRM generation improves maintainability and enables future extensions, including alternative coverage metrics, mutation tools, or entirely new minimisation strategies.

\section{Future Work}

Future improvements (detailed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion:future}) include extending the approach beyond Java, enabling multisystem minimisation for cross-project SRMs, conducting full integration testing in LASSO's Arena pipeline, benchmarking $\alpha$--$\beta$ configurations, supporting weak mutation detection, and exploring LLM-assisted test generation to close coverage or mutation gaps.

\section*{Research Questions Answered}

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1:] Greedy heuristics---especially Enhanced HGS---were identified as the most effective and practical techniques in current literature.
  \item[RQ2:] Evaluation criteria C1--C7 were defined to capture reduction, preservation, scalability, and SRM compatibility.
  \item[RQ3:] MA-EHGS emerged as the best trade-off across these criteria while preserving LASSO's language independence.
  \item[RQ4:] Integration was achieved via a requirements-matrix extension that incorporates mutation information without altering LASSO's SRM abstraction.
\end{description}

Overall, this thesis demonstrates that SRM-based minimisation can reduce test suites by roughly 45--70\% while preserving fault-detection capability. The mutation-aware MA-EHGS algorithm therefore provides a scalable and configurable mechanism for improving testing efficiency in large software repositories and strengthens the case for LASSO's behavioural abstraction as a foundation for future large-scale software analysis.
