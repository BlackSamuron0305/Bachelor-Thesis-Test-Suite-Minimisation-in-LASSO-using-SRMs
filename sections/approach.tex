\chapter{Comparative Evaluation and Selection}\label{chap:comparison}

This chapter applies the multi-criteria scoring framework established in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} to rank candidate algorithms and justify the final selection for LASSO integration. Comparative analysis of literature-reported performance across coverage preservation, reduction effectiveness, fault detection retention, computational scalability, SRM compatibility, and implementation feasibility supports the selection decision. The algorithm selected here is subsequently integrated and evaluated within LASSO.

\section{Application of Scoring Model}

The multi-criteria evaluation framework established in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} reveals that test-suite minimisation algorithms must balance competing objectives: reduction effectiveness, coverage preservation, fault detection retention, computational efficiency, and practical implementability. Systematic analysis identifies ME--OA--HGS as the optimal choice for LASSO integration based on the following considerations:

\subsection{Superior Coverage Preservation}

HGS preserves structural coverage and mutation scores by prioritising tests that cover rare requirements. This addresses a limitation of classical greedy approaches, which may select redundant tests while overlooking those with unique coverage profiles. Harrold et al.~\cite{harrold1993} demonstrated that HGS maintains 95--98\,\% coverage retention while achieving 45--70\,\% reduction. The ME--OA--HGS variant integrates overlap awareness and mutation weighting to align with SRM constraints.

\subsection{Enhanced Fault Detection Through Overlap Awareness}

The integration of overlap-aware heuristics addresses potential coverage duplication in selected tests. Overlap-aware selection algorithms consider the intersection size between candidate tests and previously selected tests, thereby maximising unique coverage acquisition. Bach et al.\ demonstrated that overlap-aware approaches achieve up to 50\,\% higher code coverage within fixed time budgets compared to classical greedy methods~\cite{bach2017}. ME--OA--HGS further incorporates mutation weighting (parameter $\beta$) to prioritise tests that contribute to mutation score retention, thereby enhancing diversity in the selected suite.

\subsection{Computational Efficiency and Scalability}

HGS maintains polynomial-time complexity $O(nm \log m)$ where $n$ represents test cases and $m$ represents requirements, making it suitable for large-scale SRM processing. The addition of overlap computation introduces bounded overhead while preserving scalability characteristics essential for industrial applications.

\subsection{SRM Compatibility and Language Independence}

Unlike approaches requiring program structural information (e.g., REDUNET's CFG analysis~\cite{mongiovi2020}), the selected approach operates exclusively on LASSO's SRM representation where stimuli represent test cases and response objects contain coverage data for executable systems. Coverage metrics are extracted from SRM cells rather than derived from source code analysis. This ensures language independence without requiring language-specific adaptations or additional structural data beyond what LASSO's execution pipeline provides.

Together, these properties make ME--OA--HGS the strongest candidate for SRM-based minimisation in LASSO.

\subsection{Empirical Evidence from Literature}

Empirical studies demonstrate fundamental trade-offs in test-suite minimisation. Rothermel et al.~\cite{rothermel1998} reported that test removal reduces execution time by 30--50\,\% but degrades fault detection by 10--20\,\%. Shi et al.~\cite{shi2018} observed Failed-Build Detection Loss (FBDL) values up to 52.2\,\% across 1{,}478 failed builds, indicating that coverage-based metrics underestimate fault loss. Noemmer and Haas~\cite{noemmer2020} reported reductions exceeding 70\,\% with an average 12.5\,\% fault-detection loss. These findings underscore the need for balancing reduction effectiveness with quality preservation.

\subsection{Empirical Motivation for Mutation-Aware Minimisation}

Empirical evidence supports integrating mutation information directly into minimisation heuristics. Jehan and Wotawa~\cite{jehan2023} achieved approximately 70\,\% reduction without measurable fault-detection degradation when mutation coverage served as the requirement metric. Conversely, Noemmer and Haas~\cite{noemmer2020} observed an average 12.5\,\% drop in mutation-based fault detection when minimising solely on structural coverage. These findings indicate that mutation data should guide the heuristic directly rather than being assessed post hoc. Consequently, HGS is extended with a $\beta$-weighted effectiveness function that incorporates mutation-kill information into the greedy selection process.

\section{Selection Decision for LASSO}

The comparative evaluation identifies ME--OA--HGS as the optimal candidate for LASSO integration. ME--OA--HGS maximises coverage preservation and fault-detection retention while achieving substantial reduction at acceptable computational complexity~\cite{harrold1993,bach2017}. Consistent with evidence from Jehan and Wotawa~\cite{jehan2023}, ME--OA--HGS extends HGS by incorporating a scalar $\beta$-weight to integrate mutation coverage directly into the selection metric alongside overlap awareness.

\section{Warnings and Limitations}

Despite its strengths, ME--OA--HGS exhibits several limitations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Overlap Computation Overhead}: Pairwise overlap estimation introduces additional computational cost; approximate or cached similarity measures may be required for very large SRMs~\cite{bach2017}.
  \item \textbf{Heuristic Sensitivity}: Effectiveness depends on parameters such as the overlap penalty $\alpha$ and optional test weights $w_i$; robust defaults and sensitivity analyses are necessary~\cite{yoo2012}.
  \item \textbf{Fault Semantics Gap}: Coverage proxies do not perfectly correlate with fault detection; mutation-aware extensions mitigate but do not eliminate this limitation~\cite{shi2018}.
  \item \textbf{SRM-Only Constraint}: Methods requiring programme structure (e.g., CFG-dependent ILPs) cannot be applied directly within LASSO's SRM-only pipeline~\cite{mongiovi2020}.
\end{itemize}
