\chapter{Comparative Evaluation and Selection}\label{chap:comparison}

This chapter applies the scoring framework from Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} to rank candidate algorithms and justify ME--OA--HGS selection for LASSO integration.

\section{Selection Rationale}

Applying criteria C1--C7 to algorithms surveyed in Chapter~\ref{chap:literature-review} identifies ME--OA--HGS as optimal. The approach combines HGS's rarity-based prioritisation~\cite{harrold1993}, overlap-aware diversity promotion~\cite{bach2017}, and mutation weighting~\cite{jehan2023} while maintaining polynomial complexity $O(nm \log m)$ and full SRM compatibility.

\paragraph{Greedy Heuristics.} Classical Greedy achieves 60--93\,\% reduction (median 67\,\%) with $O(nm)$ complexity but suffers 3.5--21\,\% mutation score loss~\cite{noemmer2020,chvatal1979}. HGS produces statistically indistinguishable results from classical greedy (performance differences within measurement error, $p \gg 0.05$)~\cite{noemmer2020,harrold1993}, achieving 0.37--2.92\,\% additional reduction at $O(nm \log m)$ cost. Delayed Greedy achieves 65--74\,\% reduction with preserved mutation scores~\cite{jehan2023,tallam2005} but incurs $O(n^2 m)$ complexity. Overlap-Aware methods demonstrate 74--77\,\% time savings in CI/CD contexts~\cite{bach2017} but lack mutation evaluation.

\paragraph{Exact Optimisation.} ILP formulations guarantee optimality but exhibit exponential worst-case complexity with double-exponential bounds $(m\Delta)^{O(m)}$~\cite{yoo2012}. REDUNET achieves up to 90\,\% reduction with 100\,\% coverage retention and greater than 3$\times$ speedup over alternatives~\cite{mongiovi2020}, but requires control-flow graphs incompatible with SRM abstraction and lacks mutation evaluation.

\paragraph{Search-Based Methods.} Genetic algorithms produce Pareto fronts for multi-objective trade-offs~\cite{yoo2012} but exhibit $O(gnm)$ complexity, parameter sensitivity, and lack systematic mutation evaluation. QIGA proposes faster convergence~\cite{hussein2020} but provides no empirical validation data.

\paragraph{ME--OA--HGS.} The proposed approach combines HGS's rarity awareness, overlap-based diversity promotion, and mutation-weighting to achieve balanced performance across all dimensions while maintaining polynomial complexity and full SRM compatibility.

\subsection{Empirical Evidence from Literature}

Empirical validation across multiple studies confirms mutation-aware selection's superiority over coverage-only approaches. Jehan and Wotawa~\cite{jehan2023} achieved approximately 70\,\% reduction with maintained mutation scores equivalent to the original suite when mutation coverage guided minimisation directly. Delayed greedy similarly preserved mutation effectiveness while achieving 65--74\,\% reduction~\cite{jehan2023}. Conversely, coverage-based minimisation exhibits systematic fault-detection degradation: Noemmer and Haas~\cite{noemmer2020} documented mutation score losses of 3.5--21\,\% (average 12.5\,\%) across seven projects despite maintaining 100\,\% structural coverage. Rothermel et al.~\cite{rothermel1998} reported consistent 10--20\,\% fault-detection degradation under adequate coverage-based reduction. 

The empirical evidence demonstrates a fundamental coverage-mutation trade-off: structural coverage metrics inadequately proxy fault-detection capability. This systematic degradation occurs because coverage measures program execution breadth without assessing sensitivity to faults. Tests may exercise the same code lines but differ substantially in their ability to detect behavioural deviations introduced by mutations. The consistent performance gap between coverage-based and mutation-aware approaches validates ME--OA--HGS's mutation-first design principle.

\section{Selection Decision}

ME--OA--HGS is selected for LASSO integration based on its optimal scoring profile. The approach extends HGS with overlap-awareness~\cite{bach2017} and mutation-weighting~\cite{jehan2023}, achieving balanced performance across all criteria while maintaining $O(nm \log m)$ scalability and full SRM compatibility.
